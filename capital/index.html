How Tezos Compares

Side-by-side comparison of Tezos with Ethereum, Solana, Cardano, and Algorand across key blockchain metrics. Available Tezos metrics update from TzKT and Octez RPC. Peer-chain values are a dated static snapshot, not equally live measurements.

  • No composite winner: Different chains optimize for different jobs and risk models
  • Block Time: Time between blocks (lower = faster)
  • Finality: Each chain's documented finality condition, not merely confirmation speed
  • Concentration: Threshold and address-versus-operator methodology must match before ranking
  • Upgrade path: Context about protocol change, not a universal numeric score

Peer values were last verified February 28, 2026 and may have changed.

How Tezos Consensus Works

Tezos uses Tenderbake, a consensus mechanism inspired by classical BFT algorithms. Tenderbake provides deterministic two-level finality when its quorum and network assumptions hold.

  • Deterministic finality: Two-level finality, normally around 12 seconds when consensus progresses on target
  • Energy efficient: Proof of Stake, not mining
  • Liquid staking: Delegate without locking tokens
  • Self-amendment: Protocol upgrades via on-chain voting
OpenTezos: Liquid PoS Technical Documentation

Tezos On-Chain Governance

Tezos can activate approved protocol changes through on-chain governance without requiring a separately maintained upgrade branch. Bakers vote on protocol proposals in a structured 5-period process.

  • Proposal: Bakers submit and upvote proposals
  • Exploration: Top proposal goes to supermajority vote
  • Cooldown: Time for testing and review
  • Promotion: Final confirmation vote
  • Adoption: New protocol activates
OpenTezos: Governance TzKT: Voting History

Tezos Economic Model

Tezos uses Adaptive Issuance—rewards automatically adjust based on staking participation to maintain optimal network security.

  • Dynamic rewards: More staking = lower issuance
  • Target: ~50% staked: System balances security vs liquidity
  • Liquidity Baking: 5 XTZ/min to XTZ/tzBTC DEX pool when active; 0 while the EMA disables the subsidy
  • Burns: Storage fees and failed ops reduce supply
Nomadic Labs: Paris OpenTezos: Economics

Adaptive Issuance

Tezos dynamically adjusts new XTZ issuance based on the staking ratio — the percentage of total supply currently reported as staked by TzKT. The gauge shows the current ratio relative to the 50% target.

  • Below target: Issuance increases to incentivize more staking
  • Above target: Issuance decreases, reducing inflation for everyone
  • At target (~50%): Optimal balance between network security and liquidity
  • Quadratic curve: Adjustments are smooth and gradual — no sudden jumps

This mechanism replaced the old fixed-rate issuance, making Tezos one of the first blockchains with a fully adaptive monetary policy. Bakers and stakers earn higher APY when participation is low, naturally pulling the ratio back toward equilibrium.

Tezos Docs: Adaptive Issuance Nomadic Labs: Paris Announcement

Protocol Continuity

Tezos has no persistent, upgrade-driven community chain split in its mainnet history. Protocol changes are adopted through on-chain governance instead of requiring users to choose between separately maintained upgrade branches. This is a historical record, not a guarantee about future consensus or availability.

  • Self-amending: Protocol changes are proposed, tested, and voted on-chain by bakers
  • One adopted protocol: No "Tezos Classic"-style upgrade branch has persisted
  • Public upgrade record: Successive amendments are recorded on-chain and documented in Protocol Anthology
  • Why it matters: A shared upgrade path reduces the risk of fragmenting users, liquidity, and developer attention
Tezos Docs: Governance Tezos.com: Governance

Tezos Mainnet Age

Tezos mainnet launched on September 17, 2018. The first-screen counter shows elapsed wall-clock time from that launch date. It is a chain-age and continuity marker, not an independently measured availability percentage or complete incident ledger.

  • Chain age: The counter is derived from the launch timestamp, not a status-page SLA
  • Upgrade history: Protocol amendments are adopted on-chain without resetting that history
  • Live liveness: The separate block ticker reports the current head and its freshness
  • Deterministic finality: Under normal quorum conditions, blocks finalize after two levels (about 12 seconds)

What is Tezos?

Tezos is a self-amending proof-of-stake blockchain. Protocol changes can be adopted through on-chain governance: bakers cast stake-weighted votes, while holders can choose the baker that represents their delegated or staked tez.
